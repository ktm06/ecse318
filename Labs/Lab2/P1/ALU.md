I utilized Yosys 0.33 and its built-in ABC mapper onto the OSU 0.5 µm standard-cell library.

I ran two models, both utilizing the Kogge-Stone adder, which is widely regarded as one of the fastest adder designs. The first approach, in ALU_16Bit_TopLevel, simply used a shared Kogge-Stone adder, where a mux picks the adder's second input (B, ~B, 0, or FFFF) based on alu_code. The second approach, in Parallel_ALU, used a parallel Kogge-Stone adder for each arithmetic operation (A + B, A - B, A + 1, and A - 1), all computing at once, with alu_code only selecting which result to output at the end.

Based on the synthesis, I found that the worst case path of the shared model runs from alu_code[3] to overflow, with a delay of 4.17 ns through 16 gates. The path first decodes alu_code into the select signal for the y mux and buffers it out to all 16 bits. It then passes through the y mux and the propagate XOR (A ^ y), through the Kogge-Stone carry tree up to bit 15, and ends at the signed overflow logic. About 1.7 ns of the delay is spent decoding and buffering alu_code before the addition even starts, while the adder itself takes about 1.8 ns. In the parallel model, the worst case path instead runs from A[1] to overflow, with a delay of 2.37 ns through 9 gates, since alu_code is no longer in front of any adder. This cuts the worst case delay by about 43%, at the cost of about 29% more area (284,850 vs 221,391) from the three extra adders.
